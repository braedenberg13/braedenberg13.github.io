01 — Objective

What I set out to answer

Simulating the kind of analysis a data analyst would run for a sales or operations team — using raw order data to surface patterns that aren't obvious from a spreadsheet.

  • Which region drives the most revenue, and by how much?
  • What are the top 10 products by total sales?
  • Which product categories and sub-categories perform best?
  • How does revenue split across Consumer, Corporate, and Home Office segments?
  • How has total revenue and order volume trended year over year?

02 — Process

Step-by-step

Each query was written from scratch in MySQL Workbench against a locally hosted database. Click any step to expand it.

Step 01 Data import & setup ▼

Created a dedicated MySQL database, defined a typed table schema matching the CSV columns, then loaded 9,800 rows using LOAD DATA INFILE.

SQL — Setup 
CREATE DATABASE superstore;
USE superstore;

CREATE TABLE orders (
  row_id        INT,
  order_id      VARCHAR(20),
  order_date    VARCHAR(20),
  ship_date     VARCHAR(20),
  ship_mode     VARCHAR(50),
  customer_name VARCHAR(100),
  segment       VARCHAR(50),
  region        VARCHAR(20),
  category      VARCHAR(50),
  sub_category  VARCHAR(50),
  product_name  VARCHAR(255),
  sales         DECIMAL(10,4)
);

LOAD DATA INFILE 'C:/ProgramData/MySQL/.../superstore.csv'
INTO TABLE orders
FIELDS TERMINATED BY ','
ENCLOSED BY '"'
LINES TERMINATED BY '\n'
IGNORE 1 ROWS;
Result
9,800 rows imported successfully across 18 columns. Zero errors, zero skipped rows.
Step 02 Query 1 — Sales by region ▼

Grouped total revenue by region using SUM() and GROUP BY to understand geographic performance distribution.

SQL — Query 1 
SELECT region, SUM(sales) AS total_sales
FROM orders
GROUP BY region
ORDER BY total_sales DESC;
Finding
West led with $710K — 82% more than the South's $389K. The two eastern regions clustered closely in the middle at $492K and $669K.
Step 03 Query 2 — Top 10 products by sales ▼

Identified the highest revenue-generating individual products using GROUP BY on product name with a LIMIT of 10.

SQL — Query 2 
SELECT product_name, SUM(sales) AS total_sales
FROM orders
GROUP BY product_name
ORDER BY total_sales DESC
LIMIT 10;
Finding
The Canon imageCLASS 2200 Copier generated $61,600 — more than double the second-place product at $27,453. The top 10 products alone accounted for a disproportionate share of total revenue.
Step 04 Query 3 — Sales by category & sub-category ▼

Used a multi-column GROUP BY to break down revenue at the sub-category level within each broader product category.

SQL — Query 3 
SELECT category, sub_category,
       SUM(sales) AS total_sales
FROM orders
GROUP BY category, sub_category
ORDER BY total_sales DESC;
Finding
Technology Phones ($327K) and Furniture Chairs ($322K) were nearly tied at the top. Office Supplies Fasteners generated only $3K — the lowest of 17 sub-categories — suggesting a candidate for de-prioritization.
Step 05 Query 4 — Sales by customer segment ▼

Analyzed how total revenue distributed across the three customer segments: Consumer, Corporate, and Home Office.

SQL — Query 4 
SELECT segment, SUM(sales) AS total_sales
FROM orders
GROUP BY segment
ORDER BY total_sales DESC;
Finding
Consumer drove $1.1M — nearly double Corporate at $688K. Home Office was the smallest at $425K. Consumer is clearly the core revenue driver and where retention efforts should focus.
Step 06 Query 5 — Year over year sales trend ▼

Used SUBSTRING() to extract the year from a text-formatted date column, then grouped revenue and unique order count by year to surface the business growth trajectory.

SQL — Query 5 
SELECT
  SUBSTRING(order_date, 7, 4) AS year,
  SUM(sales)               AS total_sales,
  COUNT(DISTINCT order_id)  AS total_orders
FROM orders
GROUP BY year
ORDER BY year ASC;
Finding
Revenue grew from $459K in 2016 to $722K in 2018 — a 57% increase. Order volume grew from 1,019 to 1,661 over the same period, confirming the growth was driven by volume, not price.
